Key Ideas · Lessons 4.1–4.2

Writing equations of lines

Lesson 4.1

Pick the form that fits what you're given

Slope-intercept, y = mx + b, when you know the y-intercept. Point-slope, y − y₁ = m(x − x₁), with any point. Simplify point-slope to slope-intercept when asked.

Example: Through (2, 7) and (4, 11): m = (11 − 7) ÷ (4 − 2) = 2. Then y − 7 = 2(x − 2), so y = 2x + 3.
Lesson 4.1

From a table

Find the slope from any two rows. If the table includes x = 0, that row gives b directly.

Example: x: 0, 1, 2 and y: 5, 3, 1 → slope −2, b = 5, so y = −2x + 5.
Lesson 4.2

Parallel and perpendicular

Parallel lines have the same slope. Perpendicular lines have negative reciprocal slopes (flip and change the sign); their slopes multiply to −1.

Example: Perpendicular to y = 3x − 1 through (3, 4): m = −1/3, so y − 4 = −⅓(x − 3) and y = −⅓x + 5.

Key Ideas · Lessons 4.3–4.5

Comparing and choosing models

Lesson 4.3

Compare in the same language

Turn each representation into a slope (rate) and a y-intercept (starting value), then compare those numbers.

Example: Plan A: $10 plus $4 per hour. Plan B passes through (0, 20) and (5, 35), so its slope is 3. Plan A grows faster; Plan B starts higher. They cost the same when 10 + 4h = 20 + 3h, at h = 10.
Lesson 4.4

Linear or nonlinear?

Linear means a constant rate of change: constant first differences, a straight-line graph, and x only to the first power.

Example: y = 2x + 9 is linear. y = 2x² + 9 and y = 2(3)x are not.
Lesson 4.5

Choose the best model

Test in order: constant first differences → linear; constant second differences → quadratic; constant ratio → exponential.

Example: Bacteria counts of 50, 100, 200, 400 each hour have a constant ratio of 2 → exponential.

Watch Out

The mistakes that cost the most points

The mistakeThe fix
Mixing the order in the slope formulaKeep the same order on top and bottom: (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁).
Not distributing in point-slope formy − 4 = 3(x − 2) becomes y − 4 = 3x − 6, then y = 3x − 2.
Perpendicular slope: flipping without changing the signDo both. The perpendicular slope to 2/5 is −5/2, not 5/2.
Using the same slope for a perpendicular lineSame slope means parallel. Perpendicular needs the negative reciprocal.
Calling a table linear after checking only the first two differencesCheck every difference in the table, not just the first pair.
Assuming every curve is exponentialRise-then-fall data is quadratic. Check second differences before ratios.

Practice Test

10 questions, every lesson mixed together

Just like a unit test, these jump between lessons. You get two tries on each question, and every question explains itself after you answer.

Question 1
What is the slope of the line through (−1, 4) and (3, −8)?
Here's why: (−8 − 4) ÷ (3 − (−1)) = −12 ÷ 4 = −3. Subtracting a negative x-value means adding: 3 − (−1) = 4.
Question 2
Write the equation of the line with slope 2 through (3, 1).
Here's why: Point-slope: y − 1 = 2(x − 3). Distribute: y − 1 = 2x − 6. Add 1: y = 2x − 5. Check: 2(3) − 5 = 1 ✓
Question 3
A table shows x: 0, 2, 4 and y: 7, 13, 19. What is the equation?
Here's why: y rises 6 each time x rises 2, so the slope is 6 ÷ 2 = 3. The row with x = 0 gives b = 7: y = 3x + 7. Using 6 as the slope forgets to divide by the change in x.
Question 4
Which line is parallel to y = −4x + 9?
Here's why: Parallel lines share a slope. Only y = −4x − 2 has slope −4; its different y-intercept makes it a separate line.
Question 5
What is the slope of a line perpendicular to y = (2/3)x + 1?
Here's why: Flip 2/3 to get 3/2, then change the sign: −3/2. Check: (2/3)(−3/2) = −1 ✓
Question 6
Which line is perpendicular to y = 2x + 3 and passes through (4, 1)?
Here's why: The perpendicular slope is −½. y − 1 = −½(x − 4) → y − 1 = −½x + 2 → y = −½x + 3. Check: −½(4) + 3 = 1 ✓. y = 2x − 7 passes through the point but is parallel, not perpendicular.
Question 7
Function A is y = 6x + 2. Function B has the table x: 0, 1, 2 and y: 10, 15, 20. Which statement is true?
Here's why: A has slope 6 and y-intercept 2. B rises 5 per step and starts at 10. So A grows faster (6 > 5) while B starts higher (10 > 2).
Question 8
Which equation is NOT linear?
Here's why: Squaring x makes the rate of change vary, so y = 3x² + 1 is quadratic. The others have x only to the first power, which gives a straight line.
Question 9
A table has x: 0, 1, 2, 3 and y: 2, 5, 10, 17. Which model fits best?
Here's why: First differences: 3, 5, 7 (not constant). Second differences: 2, 2 (constant). Constant second differences mean quadratic.
Question 10
A savings account grows by 4% every year. Which model type fits?
Here's why: Growing by a percent multiplies the balance by the same factor (1.04) every year. Repeated multiplying is exponential; adding the same dollar amount each year would be linear.
