Let's Begin

Make a variable disappear on purpose

Substitution needs one variable already isolated. Elimination doesn't — instead, you add or subtract the two whole equations so that one variable cancels out completely, leaving one simple equation behind.

When both equations are already lined up in standard form (Ax + By = C), elimination is usually the fastest of all three methods.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Add or subtract two equations to eliminate a variable
  • 2Multiply one or both equations first when coefficients don't already match
  • 3Solve for both variables and verify the solution

Vocabulary

The words behind "cancel it out"

Elimination Method

Solving a system by adding or subtracting the two equations so that one variable's coefficients cancel to zero, leaving one variable to solve for.

💡Best for: equations already in standard form, Ax + By = C.
Opposite Coefficients

A pair of matching terms, like +3y and −3y, that cancel to zero when the two equations are added together.

💡If they don't already exist, multiply one or both equations by a constant to create them.

How It Works

Line them up, cancel, solve

1
Line up both equations in standard form
Ax + By = C, with like terms stacked in matching columns.
2
Check if a variable already has opposite or matching coefficients
If not, multiply one or both entire equations by a constant to create them.
3
Add (if opposite) or subtract (if matching) the equations
One variable cancels to zero, leaving a single-variable equation.
4
Solve, then substitute back for the other variable
Plug your answer into either original equation to find the second coordinate.

💭 Notice: multiplying an entire equation by a constant doesn't change what it represents — it's still the exact same line, just written with different-sized numbers.

Real-World Context

When two totals are already given

Elimination shines whenever a real situation hands you two equations that are already balanced sums — total cost, total weight, total number of items — rather than one thing defined in terms of another.

Example: A movie theater tracks adult tickets (a) and child tickets (c) sold across two separate orders. Order 1: 3a + 2c = 44. Order 2: 3a + 5c = 65. Both start with the same 3a term — a strong hint that elimination is the fast path here.

Worked Example · Coefficients Already Match

Solving 3a + 2c = 44 and 3a + 5c = 65

Both equations already have 3a — a matching pair, so subtracting will cancel a.

1
Subtract the first equation from the second
(3a + 5c) − (3a + 2c) = 65 − 44
2
Simplify — a cancels
3c = 21 → c = 7
3
Substitute back to find a
3a + 2(7) = 44 → 3a + 14 = 44 → 3a = 30 → a = 10
4
Check in both equations
3(10)+2(7) = 44 ✓    3(10)+5(7) = 65 ✓    Solution: a = 10, c = 7

Worked Example · Multiply First

Solving 2x + 3y = 19 and x − y = 2

Neither variable has matching or opposite coefficients yet — multiply the second equation first.

1
Multiply the second equation by 3
3(x − y) = 3(2) → 3x − 3y = 6. Now y has coefficients +3 and −3 — opposites.
2
Add the two equations
(2x + 3y) + (3x − 3y) = 19 + 6 → 5x = 25 → x = 5
3
Substitute back into the simpler equation
5 − y = 2 → y = 3
4
Check in both equations
2(5)+3(3) = 19 ✓    5−3 = 2 ✓    Solution: (5, 3)

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

System: x + y = 10  &  x − y = 4

Question 1 — Adding the two equations, what do you get?

Here's why: Adding straight down: (x + x) + (y + (−y)) = 10 + 4. The y-terms cancel because they're already opposites (+y and −y), leaving 2x = 14.

Question 2 — What is the full solution point?

Here's why: From 2x = 14, x = 7. Substitute into x + y = 10: 7 + y = 10, so y = 3. Check the second equation: 7 − 3 = 4 ✓. Solution: (7, 3).

Why This Matters

A third tool for a reason

You now have three ways to solve the same kind of problem — and part of getting good at algebra is learning to glance at a system and pick the fastest one. Elimination is the go-to when equations are already lined up in standard form, since rearranging them for substitution would just waste steps.

This shows up everywhere

  • Mixing two ingredients or solutions when both totals (weight, volume, cost) are given directly.
  • Ticket sales, inventory, and revenue problems with two categories and two known totals.
  • Balancing chemical equations and other science contexts with paired totals.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
What is the goal of the elimination method?
Here's why: Elimination works by combining the two full equations (adding or subtracting) so that one variable's coefficients sum to zero, leaving a solvable one-variable equation.
Question 2
System: 2x + y = 9 and 2x − y = 3. Which operation eliminates x?
Here's why: Both equations have +2x — matching, not opposite, coefficients. Subtracting cancels matching terms; adding would only be correct if the coefficients were opposites (like +2x and −2x).
Question 3
When neither variable has matching or opposite coefficients, what should you do?
Here's why: Multiplying an entire equation by a constant doesn't change the line it represents, but it can create the matching or opposite coefficients elimination needs to work.
Question 4
After eliminating a variable, 4y = 20. What is y?
Here's why: Dividing both sides by 4 gives y = 5. This is now a simple one-variable equation, solved the same way as any other.
Question 5
Why is elimination often faster than substitution for equations already in standard form?
Here's why: Substitution requires solving one equation for a variable before you can begin. If the equations are already in standard form, elimination lets you skip straight to combining them.
