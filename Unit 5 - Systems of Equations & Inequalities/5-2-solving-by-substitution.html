Let's Begin

An exact answer, no graph needed

Graphing works, but it has a weak spot: if the solution isn't a nice whole-number point, you can only estimate where the lines cross. Substitution solves that problem completely — it gets you the exact solution every time, using pure algebra.

The idea is simple: if one equation already tells you what y equals, swap that expression into the other equation wherever you see y.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Solve a system by substituting one equation into another
  • 2Isolate a variable first when neither equation is already solved for one
  • 3Find both coordinates of the solution and check them

Vocabulary

The move behind substitution

Substitution Method

Solving a system by replacing a variable in one equation with an equivalent expression from the other equation, reducing two unknowns to one.

💡Best for: when one equation is already solved for a variable, like y = 3x + 2.
Isolate a Variable

Rearranging an equation so one variable sits alone on one side, ready to be substituted into the other equation.

💡Pick the easy one: isolate whichever variable already has a coefficient of 1, when possible.

How It Works

Four steps, every time

1
Isolate one variable in one equation
If it's not already done for you, solve one equation for x or y — pick whichever is easiest.
2
Substitute that expression into the OTHER equation
This replaces one variable with an expression made entirely of the other variable, leaving one equation with one unknown.
3
Solve for the remaining variable
Now it's a regular one-variable equation — solve it like any other.
4
Substitute back to find the other variable
Plug your answer into either original equation to find the second coordinate.

💭 Notice: you substitute into the equation you didn't use in step 1 — substituting into the same one you already solved just gives you a true-but-useless statement.

Real-World Context

When "exactly" is the requirement

Graphing a break-even point on paper is fine for estimating, but a business making a real decision needs the exact number — not "somewhere around 4.2 months." Substitution is how you get an exact, defensible answer when the outcome actually matters.

Example: A catering company charges $8 per guest plus a $150 setup fee: y = 8x + 150. A competitor charges a flat $11 per guest: y = 11x. A client wants to know the exact guest count where both quotes match — not an estimate off a graph.

Worked Example · Already Isolated

Solving y = 8x + 150 and y = 11x

Goal: Find the exact guest count where both catering quotes are equal.

1
Both equations already isolate y
Substitute the first expression for y directly into the second equation.
2
Substitute and solve for x
8x + 150 = 11x → 150 = 3x → x = 50
3
Substitute back to find y
y = 11(50) = 550
4
Check in both equations
8(50)+150 = 550 ✓    11(50) = 550 ✓    Solution: (50, 550)

Worked Example · Isolating First

Solving x + y = 12 and 2x − y = 3

Neither equation is already solved for a variable — you isolate one first.

1
Isolate y in the first equation
x + y = 12 → y = 12 − x
2
Substitute into the second equation
2x − (12 − x) = 3
3
Distribute the negative and solve
2x − 12 + x = 3 → 3x = 15 → x = 5
4
Substitute back
y = 12 − 5 = 7    Solution: (5, 7)

💭 Watch the sign: substituting (12 − x) for y means every term inside those parentheses gets the negative sign distributed across it — a very common slip-up spot.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

System: y = 4x  &  y = 2x + 10

Question 1 — Substituting 4x for y in the second equation, what does solving for x give you?

Here's why: Substitute: 4x = 2x + 10. Subtract 2x from both sides: 2x = 10. Divide by 2: x = 5. Forgetting to move the 2x term to the other side is the most common way to lose this one.

Question 2 — What is the full solution point?

Here's why: Substitute x = 5 back into the simplest equation, y = 4x: y = 4(5) = 20. Check the second equation too: 2(5)+10 = 20 ✓. The solution is (5, 20).

Why This Matters

Precision when estimation isn't good enough

Substitution is the first tool in your kit that turns "solving a system" from a visual estimate into an exact algebraic answer. Any situation where a fraction or an irrational number is the true solution — something a graph could never show precisely — substitution handles without hesitation.

This shows up everywhere

  • Pricing and break-even analysis that requires an exact number, not an estimate.
  • Mixing two solutions of different concentrations to hit a precise target.
  • Any two-condition problem where one condition can be rewritten and dropped into the other.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
What is the core move in the substitution method?
Here's why: Substitution works by swapping in an expression for a variable, collapsing the system down to one equation with one unknown you can solve directly.
Question 2
System: y = 5x and y = 2x + 9. What is x?
Here's why: 5x = 2x + 9 → 3x = 9 → x = 3. Substituting one full expression for y turns this into a simple one-variable equation.
Question 3
If neither equation is solved for a variable, what should you do first?
Here's why: Substitution requires one variable to be alone on one side before you can swap it into the other equation, so if neither equation starts that way, you isolate one first.
Question 4
In x + y = 12 solved for y, what expression replaces y?
Here's why: Subtracting x from both sides of x + y = 12 gives y = 12 − x. That full expression, parentheses and all, is what gets substituted into the other equation.
Question 5
After finding x in a system, how do you find the matching y-value?
Here's why: Once you know x, plugging it into either original equation (whichever is simpler) gives you y directly — and checking it in the other equation confirms your full solution point.
