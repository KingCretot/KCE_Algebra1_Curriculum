Key Ideas · Lesson 2.1

One-variable and compound inequalities

Lesson 2.1

Solve like an equation, with one new rule

Use the same inverse operations. If you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, flip the inequality symbol.

Example: −2x + 1 ≥ 9 → −2x ≥ 8 → x ≤ −4 (flipped because you divided by −2).
x ≤ −4: closed circle, shaded left
Lesson 2.1

Compound inequalities

Do the same step to all three parts at once. Use an open circle for < or > and a closed circle for ≤ or ≥.

Example: 3 < 2x + 5 ≤ 11 → −2 < 2x ≤ 6 → −1 < x ≤ 3.
−1 < x ≤ 3

Key Ideas · Lessons 2.2–2.3

Two-variable inequalities

Lesson 2.2

Writing from words

Build the expression, then translate the limit: at most / no more than → ≤; at least / minimum → ≥; less than / fewer than → <; more than → >.

Example: Tacos cost $3 and burritos $7, and you can spend at most $42: 3t + 7b ≤ 42.
Lesson 2.2

Writing from a graph

Read the boundary line's slope and y-intercept. A solid line means ≤ or ≥; a dashed line means < or >. Shading above means y is greater; shading below means y is less.

Example: Dashed line, y-intercept 3, slope −1, shaded below: y < −x + 3.
Lesson 2.3

Graphing: boundary, test point, shade

Graph the boundary (solid or dashed), test a point that isn't on it (usually (0, 0)), and shade the side where the test gives a true statement.

Example: y ≥ ½x − 2: solid line. Test (0, 0): 0 ≥ −2 is true, so shade the side containing the origin.
y ≥ ½x − 2: solid boundary, shaded on the origin’s side

Watch Out

The mistakes that cost the most points

The mistakeThe fix
Forgetting to flip when dividing by a negativeAny time you multiply or divide both sides by a negative, flip the symbol. Check with a number from your answer.
Flipping after adding or subtractingAdding and subtracting never flip the symbol, even when the numbers are negative.
Mixing up open and closed circlesOpen circle for < and > (not included). Closed circle for ≤ and ≥ (included).
Mixing up solid and dashed boundariesSolid for ≤ and ≥, dashed for < and > — the same idea as the circles.
Treating a false test point as "no solution"False just means the test point is on the wrong side. Shade the other side.

Practice Test

10 questions, every lesson mixed together

Just like a unit test, these jump between lessons. You get two tries on each question, and every question explains itself after you answer.

Question 1
Solve: −4x + 7 > 19
Here's why: Subtract 7: −4x > 12. Divide by −4 and flip: x < −3. Check x = −4: −4(−4) + 7 = 23, and 23 > 19 ✓
Question 2
Solve: x/3 − 2 ≤ 4
Here's why: Add 2: x/3 ≤ 6. Multiply by 3 (positive, so no flip): x ≤ 18. The choice x ≤ 6 stops before multiplying by 3.
Question 3
Solve: −5 ≤ 3x + 4 < 13
Here's why: Subtract 4 from all three parts: −9 ≤ 3x < 9. Divide all three by 3: −3 ≤ x < 3. Each end keeps its own symbol, so −3 is included and 3 is not.
Question 4
Which number line shows x < 2?
Here's why: < doesn't include 2, so the circle is open. "Less than" means smaller numbers, which are to the left.
Question 5
A ride requires riders to be at least 42 inches tall. Which inequality fits height h?
Here's why: "At least 42" means 42 inches is allowed, and anything taller too: h ≥ 42.
Question 6
Notebooks cost $4 and binders cost $6. You can spend no more than $50. Which inequality fits (n notebooks, b binders)?
Here's why: Notebooks cost 4n and binders cost 6b, so the total is 4n + 6b. "No more than $50" allows exactly $50, so the symbol is ≤.
Question 7
A graph shows a dashed line through (0, 1) with slope 3, shaded above the line. What's the inequality?
Here's why: The boundary is y = 3x + 1. Dashed means the line isn't included (< or >), and shading above means y-values greater than the line: y > 3x + 1.
Question 8
For y ≤ −2x + 5, which test-point result is correct?
Here's why: Substitute x = 0 and y = 0: 0 ≤ −2(0) + 5 becomes 0 ≤ 5, which is true. The line crosses the y-axis at 5, not at the origin, so (0, 0) is a fine test point.
Question 9
Is (2, 1) a solution of 3x − y > 4?
Here's why: Substitute: 3(2) − 1 = 6 − 1 = 5. Since 5 > 4 is true, (2, 1) is in the solution region.
Question 10
A student subtracts 6 from both sides of x + 6 > −2. Should the symbol flip?
Here's why: Subtracting 6 shifts both sides by the same amount, so their order doesn't change: x > −8. Only multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses the order.
