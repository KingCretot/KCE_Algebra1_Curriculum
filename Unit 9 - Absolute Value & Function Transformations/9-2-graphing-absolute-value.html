Let's Begin

A V instead of a U

Graph y = |x| and you get a sharp V: the outputs for −3 and 3 are both 3, and for −1 and 1 both 1. It's symmetric like a parabola, but with straight sides and a pointed vertex, because absolute value turns every negative output positive.

Today you'll graph absolute value functions and describe their key features, using the same vocabulary you used for parabolas in Unit 8.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Graph an absolute value function from a table
  • 2Identify the vertex, axis of symmetry, intercepts, domain, and range
  • 3Read the vertex from y = a|x − h| + k

Vocabulary

The V and its turning point

Absolute Value Function

f(x) = a|x − h| + k. Its graph is a V with vertex (h, k).

💡Parent function: y = |x| has its vertex at (0, 0) and sides with slopes 1 and −1.
Vertex of a V

The point where the graph changes direction. It's a minimum if a > 0 and a maximum if a < 0.

💡Example: y = −|x| + 4 has a maximum at (0, 4).

How It Works

Vertex first, then two rays

1
Find the vertex
In y = a|x − h| + k, the vertex is (h, k).
2
Make a table around it
Choose x-values on both sides of h; the outputs mirror each other.
3
Draw two rays
From the vertex, draw straight lines through the points on each side. The slopes are a and −a.
4
Name the features
Domain: all real numbers. Range: y ≥ k if a > 0, y ≤ k if a < 0. Axis of symmetry: x = h.

💭 Notice: a controls the steepness: y = 2|x| is twice as steep as y = |x|, with slopes 2 and −2.

Real-World Context

Distance over time

V-shaped graphs model distance from a fixed point over time: as you travel toward a landmark, your distance shrinks, hits zero, then grows again.

Example: A car traveling at 60 mph passes a toll booth at t = 2 hours. Its distance from the booth is d(t) = 60|t − 2| miles: 120 miles at t = 0, 0 at t = 2, and 60 miles at t = 3.

Graphing f(x) = |x − 2| − 3

Goal: Graph the function and name its key features.

1
Vertex
(2, −3), a minimum because a = 1 > 0
2
Table
x = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 → y = −1, −2, −3, −2, −1
3
Features
Axis x = 2. Range y ≥ −3. x-intercepts where |x − 2| = 3: x = −1 and x = 5. y-intercept (0, −1). Decreasing for x < 2, increasing for x > 2.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

f(x) = |x + 3| + 1

Question 1 — What is the vertex?

Here's why: In a|x − h| + k, x + 3 means h = −3, and k = 1. So the vertex is (−3, 1).

Question 2 — What is the range?

Here's why: a = 1 > 0, so the V opens up and the vertex (−3, 1) is the lowest point: y ≥ 1.

Why This Matters

The third family

The absolute value function joins lines and parabolas as the third function family in your toolkit. Next lesson, you'll see that all three shift by exactly the same rules.

This shows up everywhere

  • Distance from a point over time.
  • Graphs of error or tolerance.
  • Anything that falls to a minimum, then rises at the same rate.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
What is the vertex of y = |x − 4| + 6?
Here's why: y = a|x − h| + k has vertex (h, k): here h = 4 and k = 6.
Question 2
Which describes y = −|x| + 5?
Here's why: a = −1 < 0, so the V opens down. The vertex is (0, 5), and it's a maximum.
Question 3
What is the range of y = 3|x + 2| − 1?
Here's why: a = 3 > 0, so the vertex (−2, −1) is the lowest point. Every output is at least −1.
Question 4
What are the x-intercepts of y = |x| − 4?
Here's why: Set y = 0: |x| = 4, so x = ±4. (0, −4) is the y-intercept, not an x-intercept.
Question 5
What are the slopes of the two sides of y = 2|x − 1|?
Here's why: The a-value sets the steepness: the right side has slope 2 and the left side has slope −2.
