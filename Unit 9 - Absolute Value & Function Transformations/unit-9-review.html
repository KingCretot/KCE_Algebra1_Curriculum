Key Ideas · Lessons 9.1–9.2

Absolute value

Lesson 9.1

Isolate, check, split

Get the absolute value alone. If it equals a negative number, there's no solution. Otherwise split into two equations: inside = b or inside = −b.

Example: −3|x + 2| + 10 = 1 → |x + 2| = 3 → x = 1 or x = −5
Lesson 9.2

The V-graph

y = a|x − h| + k has vertex (h, k), axis of symmetry x = h, and slopes a and −a. a > 0 opens up (minimum); a < 0 opens down (maximum).

Example: y = |x − 2| − 3: vertex (2, −3), range y ≥ −3, zeros −1 and 5.
y = |x − 2| − 3

Key Ideas · Lessons 9.3–9.4

Transformations

Lesson 9.3

Shifts

f(x) + k moves up (k > 0) or down (k < 0). f(x + k) moves left (k > 0) or right (k < 0) — the opposite of what the sign suggests.

Example: (x − 4)² − 1 is x² moved right 4 and down 1.
Lesson 9.4

Stretches and reflections

k·f(x) stretches (|k| > 1) or compresses (|k| < 1) vertically. f(kx) compresses (|k| > 1) or stretches (|k| < 1) horizontally. A negative k reflects: outside over the x-axis, inside over the y-axis.

Example: −3|x| is |x| stretched by 3 and flipped upside down.

Practice Test

10 questions, every lesson mixed together

Just like a unit test, these jump between lessons. You get two tries on each question, and every question explains itself after you answer.

Question 1
Solve: |x − 3| = 8.
Here's why: x − 3 = 8 gives 11, and x − 3 = −8 gives −5.
Question 2
Solve: 2|x + 1| = 10.
Here's why: Divide by 2: |x + 1| = 5. Then x + 1 = 5 gives 4, and x + 1 = −5 gives −6.
Question 3
Solve: |2x| + 7 = 3.
Here's why: Subtract 7: |2x| = −4. An absolute value can't be negative, so there's no solution.
Question 4
What is the vertex of y = −|x − 1| + 6?
Here's why: a|x − h| + k has vertex (h, k): here h = 1 and k = 6. Since a < 0, it's a maximum.
Question 5
What is the range of y = 2|x| − 5?
Here's why: a = 2 > 0, so the vertex (0, −5) is the minimum and every output is at least −5.
Question 6
What are the x-intercepts of y = |x − 2| − 3?
Here's why: Set y = 0: |x − 2| = 3, so x − 2 = 3 or x − 2 = −3. That gives x = 5 and x = −1.
Question 7
How does f(x − 6) compare with f(x)?
Here's why: A change inside the function shifts horizontally, opposite the sign: − 6 moves the graph right 6.
Question 8
Which equation is y = x² shifted left 2 and down 7?
Here's why: Left 2 means x + 2 inside; down 7 means − 7 outside.
Question 9
How does g(x) = −2f(x) compare with f(x)?
Here's why: The −2 is outside, so it acts on the outputs: the 2 stretches vertically and the negative reflects over the x-axis.
Question 10
The point (4, −2) is on the graph of f. Which point must be on y = f(2x)?
Here's why: f(2x) gives f's outputs at half the x-value: at x = 2, f(2 · 2) = f(4) = −2. So (2, −2).
