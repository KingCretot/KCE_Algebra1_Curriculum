Let's Begin

Drawing a line through a cloud

Plot each student's study hours against their test score and you get a cloud of points — not a perfect line, but clearly drifting upward. A line of fit captures that drift in one equation, and its slope tells you how much a score tends to rise with each extra hour.

Today you'll fit a linear function to real data, interpret its slope and y-intercept, and use it to make predictions — including knowing when not to trust one.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Create and read a scatter plot of bivariate numerical data
  • 2Fit a linear function to the data and write its equation
  • 3Interpret the slope and y-intercept in context and use the model to predict

Vocabulary

Points and the line through them

Scatter Plot

A graph of bivariate numerical data: each point is one individual, plotted as (x, y).

💡Example: (hours studied, test score) for each student in a class.
Line of Fit

A line that follows the trend of a scatter plot, with about as many points above it as below. The least-squares regression line is the best-fitting one.

💡Use it to: predict y for an x-value inside the range of the data.

How It Works

Plot, fit, interpret

1
Plot the points
Put the explanatory variable (the one you think influences the other) on the x-axis.
2
Draw a line through the trend
Balance the points above and below; the line doesn't have to pass through any data point.
3
Write the equation
Use two points on your line to find the slope, then y = mx + b. Technology gives the least-squares line directly.
4
Interpret and predict
Slope: the change in y per 1-unit increase in x. y-intercept: the predicted y when x = 0, if that makes sense.

💭 Notice: Predicting inside the range of the data (interpolation) is reasonable. Predicting far outside it (extrapolation) is risky — the trend may not continue.

Real-World Context

From messy data to a prediction

Lines of fit turn real, messy data into usable predictions: sales from advertising, fuel cost from distance, or growth over time.

Example: A line of fit for a used car's value is V = −1,800t + 22,000, where t is its age in years. The slope means the car loses about $1,800 in value per year, and the y-intercept estimates a new car's value at $22,000.

Worked Example · Fitting a Line

Study hours and test scores

Goal: Six students: (1, 62), (2, 68), (3, 71), (4, 77), (5, 80), (6, 86). Fit a line and interpret it.

1
Plot and observe
The points rise steadily from left to right: a positive linear trend.
2
Fit the line
Technology gives the least-squares line y ≈ 4.6x + 57.8. A line drawn by eye through the trend gives nearly the same result.
3
Interpret
Each extra hour of study is associated with about 4.6 more points. The y-intercept predicts about 58 points for a student who doesn't study at all.
Study hours vs. test score, with y ≈ 4.6x + 57.8

Worked Example · Predicting

Using y ≈ 4.6x + 57.8

Goal: Predict scores for 3.5 hours and for 20 hours of study.

1
Predict for 3.5 hours
4.6(3.5) + 57.8 = 73.9, about 74 points
2
Predict for 20 hours
4.6(20) + 57.8 = 149.8 — impossible on a 100-point test
3
Interpret
3.5 hours is inside the range of the data, so that prediction is reasonable. 20 hours is far outside it, so the model breaks down.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

A line of fit for a plant's height is h = 1.5w + 4, where w is weeks and h is centimeters.

Question 1 — What does the slope 1.5 mean?

Here's why: Slope is the change in h for each 1-week increase: about 1.5 cm of growth per week. The starting height is the y-intercept, 4 cm.

Question 2 — What height does the model predict at 6 weeks?

Here's why: h = 1.5(6) + 4 = 9 + 4 = 13 cm. 9 cm leaves out the starting height of 4 cm.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
On a scatter plot of age (x) and reaction time (y), what does each point represent?
Here's why: Each point is one individual, plotted with both of their values.
Question 2
A line of fit is y = 12x + 20, where x is hours worked and y is dollars earned. What does 12 mean?
Here's why: The slope is the change in y per 1-unit change in x: about $12 more per extra hour.
Question 3
Using y = 12x + 20, predict earnings for 5 hours.
Here's why: y = 12(5) + 20 = 60 + 20 = $80. $60 leaves out the 20.
Question 4
Data were collected for x = 1 to 10. Which prediction is least reliable?
Here's why: x = 50 is far outside the data's range. That's extrapolation, and the trend may not continue that far.
Question 5
A good line of fit should…
Here's why: A line of fit captures the overall trend. It doesn't need to hit any particular point.
