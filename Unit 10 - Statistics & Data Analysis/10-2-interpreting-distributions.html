Let's Begin

Center, spread, shape

Two classes both average 80 on a test. In one, almost everyone scored between 75 and 85. In the other, scores ran from 40 to 100. Same average, very different stories. A distribution's center is only part of it — spread and shape matter just as much.

Today you'll describe distributions by their center, spread, and shape, and see how an outlier changes the summary.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Describe a distribution's center, spread, and shape
  • 2Read and interpret box plots
  • 3Identify outliers and explain their effect on the mean and median

Vocabulary

The words for describing data

Center and Spread

Center: the mean (average) or median (middle value). Spread: the range (max − min) or interquartile range (IQR = Q3 − Q1).

💡Which to use: for skewed data or data with outliers, the median and IQR describe a typical value better.
Shape and Outliers

Shape: symmetric, skewed right (a long tail toward high values), or skewed left. An outlier is a value far from the rest — commonly, more than 1.5 · IQR below Q1 or above Q3.

💡Example: incomes are usually skewed right: most are moderate, and a few are very high.

How It Works

Summarize, describe, check

1
Find the five-number summary
Minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum. These five numbers build a box plot.
2
Describe the shape
Symmetric, skewed left, or skewed right. Look for clusters and gaps too.
3
Choose the right center
Symmetric → mean. Skewed or with outliers → median and IQR.
4
Check for outliers
Anything below Q1 − 1.5 · IQR or above Q3 + 1.5 · IQR.

💭 Notice: An outlier pulls the mean toward it but barely moves the median. That's why news reports use median home prices, not mean prices.

Real-World Context

Summaries that can mislead

Reports summarize distributions constantly: median household income, average temperature, the range of prices. Knowing which summary fits keeps you from being misled.

Example: Five employees earn $40k, $42k, $45k, $48k, and $250k. The mean is $85k, but the median is $45k — a much better picture of a typical salary.

Worked Example · Box Plot

Reading a box plot of quiz scores

Goal: Ten quiz scores: 12, 14, 15, 15, 16, 17, 18, 18, 19, 20. Summarize the distribution.

1
Five-number summary
Min 12, Q1 15, median 16.5, Q3 18, max 20
2
Spread
Range: 20 − 12 = 8. IQR: 18 − 15 = 3, so the middle half of the scores lies within 3 points.
3
Shape
The lower part of the data is more spread out (12 to 16.5) than the upper part (16.5 to 20), so the distribution is slightly skewed left.

Worked Example · Outliers

The effect of one outlier

Goal: Wait times (minutes): 4, 5, 5, 6, 7, 8, 30. Find the mean and median, and test 30.

1
Mean and median
Mean = 65 ÷ 7 ≈ 9.3 minutes. Median = 6 minutes.
2
Outlier test
Q1 = 5 and Q3 = 8, so IQR = 3. Upper fence: 8 + 4.5 = 12.5. Since 30 > 12.5, 30 is an outlier.
3
Interpret
Without 30, the mean drops to about 5.8 and the median to 5.5. The outlier moved the mean by more than 3 minutes but the median by only half a minute, so the median better describes a typical wait.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Data: 2, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6, 22

Question 1 — Which is the better measure of center?

Here's why: 22 is far from the rest and pulls the mean up to about 6.4, higher than six of the seven values. The median, 4, describes a typical value better.

Question 2 — Is 22 an outlier?

Here's why: Q1 = 3 and Q3 = 6, so IQR = 3 and the upper fence is 6 + 4.5 = 10.5. 22 is above the fence, so it's an outlier.

Why This Matters

Every statistic is a choice

Every statistic you read — average, median, typical, range — is a choice about how to summarize a distribution. Knowing the choices lets you tell when one is misleading.

This shows up everywhere

  • Comparing test results between classes.
  • Understanding median income and home prices.
  • Spotting a misleading average.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
Which measure of center is most affected by an outlier?
Here's why: The mean uses every value's size, so one extreme value pulls it. The median depends only on the middle position.
Question 2
A histogram has a long tail to the right. What is its shape?
Here's why: A distribution is skewed in the direction of its long tail. A tail toward high values means skewed right.
Question 3
A box plot shows Q1 = 20 and Q3 = 35. What is the IQR?
Here's why: IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 35 − 20 = 15. It measures the spread of the middle half of the data.
Question 4
Q1 = 10 and Q3 = 18. Which value is an outlier?
Here's why: IQR = 8, so the fences are 10 − 12 = −2 and 18 + 12 = 30. Only 31 falls outside them.
Question 5
Data: 3, 5, 6, 6, 8, 9, 12. What is the median?
Here's why: The data are already in order, and the middle (4th of 7) value is 6. 7 is the mean, and 9 is the range.
