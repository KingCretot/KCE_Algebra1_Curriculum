Let's Begin

Multiplying, run backward

Lesson 7.2 took (x + 3)(x + 2) and turned it into x² + 5x + 6. Factoring goes the other way: start with the polynomial and find what was multiplied to make it. The first and easiest tool is pulling out whatever every term has in common.

Every factoring problem starts by looking for a greatest common factor. Today you'll factor out GCFs, then use grouping to factor polynomials with four terms.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Find the greatest common factor (GCF) of a polynomial's terms
  • 2Factor out a GCF, including a negative GCF
  • 3Factor a four-term polynomial by grouping

Vocabulary

Undoing multiplication

Factoring

Rewriting a polynomial as a product of simpler polynomials. Factoring undoes multiplying.

💡Example: 6x² + 9x = 3x(2x + 3).
Greatest Common Factor (GCF)

The largest monomial that divides every term: the GCF of the coefficients times the lowest power of each shared variable.

💡Example: For 8x³ and 12x², the GCF is 4x².

How It Works

Find it, divide it out, check

1
Find the GCF of the coefficients
For 18x⁴ − 12x³ + 6x², the GCF of 18, 12, and 6 is 6.
2
Find the lowest power of each shared variable
Every term has x, and the lowest power is x². So the GCF is 6x².
3
Divide each term by the GCF
That's Lesson 7.3: 18x⁴/6x² − 12x³/6x² + 6x²/6x² = 3x² − 2x + 1.
4
Write the product and check
6x²(3x² − 2x + 1). Multiply back to check.

💭 Notice: When the leading coefficient is negative, it's usually cleaner to factor out a negative GCF: −4x² + 8x = −4x(x − 2).

Real-World Context

Factored form shows structure

Factored form reveals things that expanded form hides. In Unit 8 you'll use it to find where a thrown ball lands; even today it makes an expression easier to read.

Example: A ball tossed upward is h = −16t² + 32t feet high after t seconds. Factored, h = −16t(t − 2), which makes it clear that the height is 0 at t = 0 (the toss) and at t = 2 (the landing).

Worked Example · GCF

Factoring 12x³y − 18x²y²

Goal: Factor out the greatest common factor.

1
GCF of the coefficients
The GCF of 12 and 18 is 6.
2
Lowest powers of the shared variables
x² and y, so the GCF is 6x²y.
3
Divide and write the product
12x³y/6x²y = 2x and −18x²y²/6x²y = −3y, so 6x²y(2x − 3y). Check: 6x²y · 2x = 12x³y ✓ and 6x²y · (−3y) = −18x²y² ✓

Worked Example · Grouping

Factoring x³ + 4x² + 3x + 12

Goal: Factor the four-term polynomial by grouping.

1
Group into pairs
(x³ + 4x²) + (3x + 12)
2
Factor the GCF of each pair
x²(x + 4) + 3(x + 4)
3
Factor out the shared binomial
(x + 4)(x² + 3). Check: x³ + 3x + 4x² + 12 ✓

💭 Notice: Grouping works only when both pairs leave the same binomial. If they don't match, try factoring a negative out of the second pair.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Factor: 2x³ − 6x² + 5x − 15

Question 1 — After grouping, what do the two pairs factor to?

Here's why: 2x³ − 6x² has GCF 2x², leaving x − 3. 5x − 15 has GCF 5, also leaving x − 3. Both pairs leave the same binomial, which is what grouping needs.

Question 2 — What is the fully factored form?

Here's why: Factor out the shared (x − 3): (x − 3)(2x² + 5). Check: 2x³ + 5x − 6x² − 15 ✓. The 5 stays positive because 5x − 15 = +5(x − 3).

Why This Matters

The gateway to solving quadratics

Factoring is how you'll solve many quadratic equations in Unit 8. Every factoring problem starts the same way — check for a GCF — so building that habit now pays off on every problem after this.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
What is the GCF of 15x⁴ and 25x²?
Here's why: The GCF of 15 and 25 is 5, and the lowest power of x is x². So the GCF is 5x².
Question 2
Factor completely: 9x² + 12x.
Here's why: The GCF is 3x: 9x²/3x = 3x and 12x/3x = 4. The choices 3(3x² + 4x) and x(9x + 12) are factored, but not completely — each still has a common factor inside.
Question 3
Factor: −5x² + 20x.
Here's why: Factor out −5x: −5x²/(−5x) = x and 20x/(−5x) = −4. So −5x(x − 4). Check: −5x · x = −5x² ✓ and −5x · (−4) = 20x ✓
Question 4
Factor by grouping: x³ + 2x² + 7x + 14.
Here's why: x²(x + 2) + 7(x + 2) = (x + 2)(x² + 7). Check: x³ + 7x + 2x² + 14 ✓
Question 5
A ball's height is h = −16t² + 48t. Which is the factored form?
Here's why: GCF −16t: −16t²/(−16t) = t and 48t/(−16t) = −3. So h = −16t(t − 3), which shows the ball lands at t = 3 seconds.
