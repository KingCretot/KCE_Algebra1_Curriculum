Let's Begin

Patterns that factor in one step

Multiply (x + 5)(x − 5) and the middle terms cancel: x² − 5x + 5x − 25 = x² − 25. Run that backward and any difference of two perfect squares factors instantly. Spotting a pattern saves you the whole number puzzle.

Today you'll learn to recognize two patterns: the difference of squares and the perfect square trinomial.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Factor a difference of two squares
  • 2Recognize and factor a perfect square trinomial
  • 3Factor completely, taking out a GCF first when there is one

Vocabulary

Two patterns worth memorizing

Difference of Squares

a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b). Two perfect squares with a minus sign between them.

💡Example: x² − 49 = (x + 7)(x − 7).
Perfect Square Trinomial

a² + 2ab + b² = (a + b)² and a² − 2ab + b² = (a − b)². The first and last terms are perfect squares, and the middle term is twice their product.

💡Example: x² + 10x + 25 = (x + 5)², because 2 · x · 5 = 10x.

How It Works

Spot the pattern

1
Check for a GCF
2x² − 18 = 2(x² − 9). The pattern often appears only after the GCF is out.
2
Two terms with a minus sign?
If both are perfect squares, use a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b).
3
Three terms with square ends?
Check whether the middle term is 2ab. If it is, the trinomial is (a + b)² or (a − b)², matching the middle sign.
4
Check by multiplying
(x + 7)(x − 7) = x² − 49 ✓

💭 Notice: A sum of squares, like x² + 49, does not factor over the real numbers. Only a difference of squares does.

Real-World Context

Patterns in area and mental math

The difference of squares appears whenever you subtract one square area from another, and it turns some multiplication into easy mental math.

Example: A square frame is x inches on a side, with a square opening 4 inches on a side. The frame's area is x² − 16 = (x + 4)(x − 4) square inches. And for mental math: 31 × 29 = (30 + 1)(30 − 1) = 900 − 1 = 899.

Worked Example · Difference of Squares

Factoring 9x² − 64

Goal: Factor completely.

1
Identify the squares
9x² = (3x)² and 64 = 8²
2
Apply the pattern
(3x + 8)(3x − 8)
3
Check
9x² − 24x + 24x − 64 = 9x² − 64 ✓

Worked Example · Perfect Square Trinomial

Factoring x² − 12x + 36

Goal: Factor completely.

1
Check the ends
x² = (x)² and 36 = 6²
2
Check the middle
2 · x · 6 = 12x, and the middle sign is negative
3
Write the square
(x − 6)². Check: x² − 6x − 6x + 36 = x² − 12x + 36 ✓

💭 Notice: If the middle term isn't exactly 2ab, it isn't a perfect square trinomial. Fall back to the product-and-sum method from Lesson 7.5.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Factor completely: 5x² − 20

Question 1 — What is the first step?

Here's why: Always check for a GCF first: 5x² − 20 = 5(x² − 4). The second choice multiplies out to 5x² + 15x − 20, not 5x² − 20.

Question 2 — What is the complete factorization?

Here's why: x² − 4 is a difference of squares: (x + 2)(x − 2). So 5x² − 20 = 5(x + 2)(x − 2). 5(x − 2)² expands to 5x² − 20x + 20.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
Factor: x² − 81.
Here's why: x² and 81 are perfect squares with a minus between them: (x + 9)(x − 9). (x − 9)² would have a middle term of −18x.
Question 2
Factor: 4x² − 25.
Here's why: 4x² = (2x)² and 25 = 5², so 4x² − 25 = (2x + 5)(2x − 5).
Question 3
Which is a perfect square trinomial?
Here's why: x² and 49 are squares, and 2 · x · 7 = 14x matches the middle term, so it equals (x + 7)². x² + 7x + 49 has the wrong middle term.
Question 4
Factor: x² − 8x + 16.
Here's why: 16 = 4² and 2 · x · 4 = 8x with a minus sign, so x² − 8x + 16 = (x − 4)². (x + 4)(x − 4) is x² − 16.
Question 5
Factor completely: 3x² − 27.
Here's why: GCF first: 3(x² − 9). Then x² − 9 = (x + 3)(x − 3). 3(x² − 9) isn't finished, and (3x + 9)(x − 3) still has a 3 inside the first factor.
