Key Ideas · Lessons 7.1–7.3

Operations on polynomials

Lesson 7.1

Add and subtract like terms

Write in standard form, then combine only like terms. When subtracting, flip every sign in the second polynomial.

Example: (6x² − x + 2) − (2x² + 4x − 5) = 6x² − x + 2 − 2x² − 4x + 5 = 4x² − 5x + 7
Lesson 7.2

Multiply every term by every term

Distribute, multiply coefficients, add exponents, then combine like terms. An area model keeps the products organized.

Example: (x − 5)(x + 2) = x² + 2x − 5x − 10 = x² − 3x − 10
(x − 5)(x + 2) as an area model
Lesson 7.3

Divide every term by the monomial

Split into one fraction per term and use the quotient rule. A term divided by itself is 1.

Example: (20x³ − 12x² + 4x) ÷ 4x = 5x² − 3x + 1

Key Ideas · Lessons 7.4–7.6

Factoring

Lesson 7.4

GCF first, grouping for four terms

Pull out the greatest common factor. For four terms, factor each pair and then the shared binomial.

Example: x³ + 4x² + 3x + 12 = x²(x + 4) + 3(x + 4) = (x + 4)(x² + 3)
Lesson 7.5

Trinomials: product and sum

For x² + bx + c, find numbers with product c and sum b. For ax² + bx + c, use product a · c, split the middle, and group.

Example: x² − 2x − 15 = (x + 3)(x − 5); 2x² + 7x + 3 = (2x + 1)(x + 3)
Lesson 7.6

Special patterns

a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b). a² ± 2ab + b² = (a ± b)². A sum of squares doesn't factor.

Example: 9x² − 64 = (3x + 8)(3x − 8); x² − 12x + 36 = (x − 6)²

Key Ideas · Lesson 7.7

Radicals

Lesson 7.7

Simplify, then operate

Pull out perfect-square (or perfect-cube) factors. Add only like radicals. Multiply or divide under one radical, then simplify.

Example: √12 + √75 = 2√3 + 5√3 = 7√3; √8 · √6 = √48 = 4√3

Watch Out

The mistakes that cost the most points

The mistakeThe fix
Not flipping every sign when subtracting−(2x² − 4x + 6) = −2x² + 4x − 6. Every term changes sign.
Adding exponents when adding terms: x² + x² → x⁴Adding like terms changes only the coefficient: 2x².
Skipping the middle terms: (x + 5)² → x² + 25(x + 5)² = x² + 10x + 25. Multiply every term by every term.
Dropping the 1 when dividing: 4x ÷ 4x → 0A term divided by itself is 1.
Stopping before factoring completelyCheck for a GCF first, and check every factor again: 3x² − 27 = 3(x + 3)(x − 3).
Picking a pair with the right product but wrong sumCheck both: product c and sum b. 3 and 4 multiply to 12 but add to 7, not 8.
Adding under the radical: √12 + √75 → √87Simplify each radical, then add like radicals: 7√3.

Practice Test

10 questions, every lesson mixed together

Just like a unit test, these jump between lessons. You get two tries on each question, and every question explains itself after you answer.

Question 1
Subtract: (7x² − 3x + 2) − (4x² − 5x − 1).
Here's why: Flip every sign in the second polynomial: −4x² + 5x + 1. Then 3x² + 2x + 3. The choice 3x² − 8x + 1 didn't flip any signs.
Question 2
Multiply: (x − 6)(x + 4).
Here's why: x² + 4x − 6x − 24 = x² − 2x − 24. x² − 24 skips the outer and inner products.
Question 3
Multiply: (3x − 2)².
Here's why: (3x − 2)(3x − 2) = 9x² − 6x − 6x + 4 = 9x² − 12x + 4. Squaring each term separately misses the middle term.
Question 4
Divide: (18x⁴ − 12x³ + 6x) ÷ 6x.
Here's why: 18x⁴/6x = 3x³, −12x³/6x = −2x², and 6x/6x = 1. The 1 is easy to drop.
Question 5
Factor completely: 8x³ − 12x².
Here's why: The GCF is 4x². The choices 4x(2x² − 3x) and 2x²(4x − 6) still have common factors inside the parentheses.
Question 6
Factor: x² + x − 30.
Here's why: Product −30 and sum +1: 6 and −5. (x − 6)(x + 5) gives a middle term of −x.
Question 7
Factor: 2x² + 5x − 3.
Here's why: a · c = −6, and 6 + (−1) = 5. Split: 2x² + 6x − x − 3 = 2x(x + 3) − 1(x + 3) = (2x − 1)(x + 3).
Question 8
Factor completely: 2x² − 50.
Here's why: GCF first: 2(x² − 25). Then the difference of squares: 2(x + 5)(x − 5).
Question 9
Simplify √45 + √20.
Here's why: √45 = 3√5 and √20 = 2√5, so the sum is 5√5. √65 adds under the radical, which isn't allowed.
Question 10
Simplify √6 · √15.
Here's why: √6 · √15 = √90 = √(9 · 10) = 3√10. √21 adds instead of multiplying.
