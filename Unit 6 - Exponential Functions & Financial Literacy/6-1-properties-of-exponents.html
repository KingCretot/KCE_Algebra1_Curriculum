Let's Begin

Exponents are shorthand — and the shorthand has rules

Writing x · x · x · x · x is tiring. Writing x5 is not. Exponents are shorthand for repeated multiplication — and once you see that, no exponent rule needs to be memorized. You can rebuild any of them in ten seconds by writing the multiplication out.

Today's rules let you simplify expressions like (2x3)2 · x4 in a few clean steps instead of writing out a long string of factors. You'll use them for every exponential function in this unit.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Apply the product, quotient, and power rules for exponents
  • 2Explain why a0 = 1 and what a negative exponent means
  • 3Simplify algebraic expressions using several rules together

Vocabulary

The parts of a power

Base and Exponent

In an, the base a is the number being multiplied, and the exponent n tells how many times it appears as a factor.

💡Example: 34 = 3 · 3 · 3 · 3 = 81. The base is 3 and the exponent is 4.
Zero and Negative Exponents

For any nonzero a: a0 = 1, and a−n = 1/an. A negative exponent means “one over” — it does not make the number negative.

💡Example: 5−2 = 1/52 = 1/25, a small positive number.

How It Works

Five rules, each one easy to rebuild

1
Product rule: am · an = am+n
Same base multiplied → add the exponents. x2 · x3 = (x · x)(x · x · x) = x5.
2
Quotient rule: am ÷ an = am−n
Same base divided → subtract the exponents. x7 ÷ x3 = x4, because three x's cancel.
3
Power of a power: (am)n = amn
A power raised to a power → multiply the exponents. (x2)3 = x2 · x2 · x2 = x6.
4
Power of a product: (ab)n = anbn
The outside exponent applies to every factor inside. (3x)2 = 9x2, not 3x2.
5
Zero and negative exponents
a0 = 1 and a−n = 1/an. Write final answers with positive exponents.

💭 Notice: a0 = 1 comes straight from the quotient rule: x3 ÷ x3 = x0, and any nonzero number divided by itself is 1.

Real-World Context

Why scientists and engineers lean on these rules

Very large and very small numbers — the distance to the Sun, the width of a virus — are written in scientific notation, which is built on powers of 10. Multiplying or dividing them is exactly the product and quotient rules.

Example: Light travels about 3 × 105 km every second. In 103 seconds (about 17 minutes) it travels 3 × 105 × 103 = 3 × 108 km. The product rule turned the multiplication into adding 5 + 3.

Worked Example · Several Rules

Simplifying (2x3)2 · x4

Goal: Write (2x3)2 · x4 as a single term.

1
Power of a product
(2x3)2 = 22 · (x3)2 = 4x6
2
Product rule
4x6 · x4 = 4x6+4 = 4x10
3
Check with a number
Try x = 2: (2 · 8)2 · 16 = 256 · 16 = 4,096, and 4(2)10 = 4(1,024) = 4,096 ✓

💭 Notice: The 2 got squared too. Forgetting to apply the outside exponent to the coefficient is the most common slip in this lesson.

Worked Example · Negative Exponents

Simplifying 12x2y5 ÷ 4x6y2

Goal: Simplify 12x2y5 ÷ 4x6y2 using positive exponents only.

1
Divide the coefficients
12 ÷ 4 = 3
2
Quotient rule on each variable
x2−6 = x−4 and y5−2 = y3
3
Rewrite the negative exponent
3x−4y3 = 3y3 / x4

💭 Notice: Only x−4 moved to the bottom. The 3 and y3 already had positive exponents, so they stay on top.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Simplify: (x4)3 · x−2

Question 1 — What is (x4)3?

Here's why: Power of a power: multiply the exponents, 4 × 3 = 12. x7 adds them — adding is the product rule, which is for multiplying two powers, not raising a power to a power.

Question 2 — Now multiply x12 · x−2. What is the simplified result?

Here's why: Same base multiplied, so add the exponents: 12 + (−2) = 10. x−24 multiplies the exponents, which only happens with a power of a power.

Why This Matters

These rules power the rest of the unit

Every exponential function in this unit — population growth, compound interest, a medicine leaving the body — is built from powers. Simplifying them, comparing them, and rewriting them all depend on today's five rules.

This shows up everywhere

  • Scientific notation in science classes: multiplying and dividing powers of 10.
  • Computer storage: 210 bytes in a kilobyte, 220 in a megabyte.
  • Finance: compound interest formulas built from powers.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
Simplify x5 · x3.
Here's why: Same base multiplied: add the exponents, 5 + 3 = 8. Multiplying the exponents (15) is the power-of-a-power rule, which doesn't apply here.
Question 2
Simplify (3a2)3.
Here's why: The exponent 3 applies to both factors: 33 = 27 and (a2)3 = a6. 3a6 forgets to cube the 3.
Question 3
What is 4−2?
Here's why: A negative exponent means one over: 4−2 = 1/42 = 1/16. It's a small positive number, not a negative one.
Question 4
Simplify 18m7 ÷ 6m3.
Here's why: Divide the coefficients (18 ÷ 6 = 3) and subtract the exponents (7 − 3 = 4): 3m4.
Question 5
Which expression equals 1 for every nonzero value of x?
Here's why: Any nonzero number to the zero power is 1. The quotient rule shows why: x3 ÷ x3 = x0, and a number divided by itself is 1.
