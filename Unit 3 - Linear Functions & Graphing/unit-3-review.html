Key Ideas · Lessons 3.1–3.2

Functions and how we write them

Lesson 3.1

What makes a function

Each input has exactly one output. On a graph, no vertical line crosses the graph more than once.

Example: {(1, 4), (2, 4), (3, 7)} is a function — two inputs sharing an output is fine. {(1, 4), (1, 6)} is not, because input 1 has two outputs.
Lesson 3.1

Classify from a table

With evenly spaced x-values: constant first differences → linear; constant second differences → quadratic; constant ratio → exponential.

Example: y = 5, 8, 11, 14 has differences of 3 → linear. y = 2, 6, 18, 54 has a ratio of 3 → exponential.
Lesson 3.2

Function notation

f(x) is the output when the input is x. To evaluate, replace every x with the input.

Example: f(x) = 3x − 4: f(5) = 3(5) − 4 = 11, so the point (5, 11) is on the graph.

Key Ideas · Lessons 3.3–3.5

Rate of change, graphs, and real situations

Lesson 3.3

Average rate of change

[f(b) − f(a)] ÷ (b − a): change in output over change in input. For a linear function, it is the same on every interval — the slope.

Example: h(1) = 4 cm and h(5) = 20 cm: (20 − 4) ÷ (5 − 1) = 4 cm per week.
Lesson 3.4

Key features of a line

y-intercept: set x = 0. x-intercept: set y = 0 and solve. Rate of change: the coefficient of x. Domain and range: all real numbers unless the context limits them.

Example: f(x) = −2x + 6 has y-intercept (0, 6), x-intercept (3, 0), and rate of change −2.
f(x) = −2x + 6
Lesson 3.5

Real-world models need constraints

Starting value + rate × input builds the model. Then restrict the domain to inputs that make sense.

Example: A 500-gallon tank drains 25 gallons per minute: V(t) = 500 − 25t. It's empty at t = 20, so the realistic domain is 0 ≤ t ≤ 20.

Watch Out

The mistakes that cost the most points

The mistakeThe fix
Reading f(x) as "f times x"f(x) means "the output of f at x." It's a name, not multiplication.
Calling a relation "not a function" because two inputs share an outputSharing an output is fine. One input with two different outputs is what breaks a function.
Forgetting to divide by the change in xRate of change is a fraction: change in output ÷ change in input.
Writing intercepts backwardThe y-intercept is always (0, y) and the x-intercept is always (x, 0).
Sign slips with negative inputsUse parentheses when you substitute: f(−3) = −2(−3) + 1 = 6 + 1 = 7.
Reporting answers outside the realistic domainIf a model predicts negative gallons or negative hours, the input is outside where the model applies.

Practice Test

10 questions, every lesson mixed together

Just like a unit test, these jump between lessons. You get two tries on each question, and every question explains itself after you answer.

Question 1
Which relation is NOT a function?
Here's why: In {(2, 5), (3, 7), (2, 9)}, the input 2 goes to two different outputs, 5 and 9. That breaks the rule. The other sets only repeat outputs, which is allowed.
Question 2
A table has x: 0, 1, 2, 3 and y: 1, 4, 9, 16. Which type of function is it?
Here's why: First differences are 3, 5, 7 — not constant, so it isn't linear. Second differences are 2, 2 — constant, so it's quadratic.
Question 3
A table has x: 0, 1, 2, 3 and y: 400, 200, 100, 50. Which type of function is it?
Here's why: Each y-value is half the one before, a constant ratio of ½. A constant ratio means exponential (here, decay).
Question 4
If h(x) = x² − 3x, what is h(−2)?
Here's why: Substitute with parentheses: h(−2) = (−2)² − 3(−2) = 4 + 6 = 10. Squaring a negative gives a positive, and −3 times −2 is +6.
Question 5
C(n) = 12n + 30 gives the cost in dollars of n concert tickets, including a booking fee. What does C(4) = 78 mean?
Here's why: The input (4) is the number of tickets and the output (78) is the total cost: 12(4) + 30 = 48 + 30 = 78 ✓
Question 6
A phone battery is at 90% at 1 p.m. and 54% at 5 p.m. What is the average rate of change?
Here's why: (54 − 90) ÷ (5 − 1) = −36 ÷ 4 = −9. The battery dropped an average of 9 points each hour; the negative sign shows it is decreasing. −36 is the total change, not the rate.
Question 7
What is the x-intercept of y = 5x − 15?
Here's why: Set y = 0: 0 = 5x − 15, so 5x = 15 and x = 3. The x-intercept is (3, 0); (0, −15) is the y-intercept.
Question 8
Which statement about f(x) = −4x + 2 is true?
Here's why: The rate of change is −4, so the line drops 4 for every step right. Its y-intercept is (0, 2), and its x-intercept is (0.5, 0) since −4x + 2 = 0 gives x = 0.5.
Question 9
A delivery driver earns P(h) = 18h for h hours, and shifts last at most 10 hours. What is the realistic domain?
Here's why: Hours can't be negative, and a shift tops out at 10 hours, so 0 ≤ h ≤ 10. The 180 is the most the driver can earn — that's the range, not the domain.
Question 10
A 30 cm candle burns 2.5 cm per hour. Which model gives its height after t hours, and when does it burn out?
Here's why: Start at 30 and subtract 2.5 every hour: H(t) = 30 − 2.5t. Burned out means H(t) = 0: 2.5t = 30, so t = 12 hours.
