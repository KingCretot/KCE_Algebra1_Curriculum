Let's Begin

Every key feature has a meaning

In a real problem, the vertex is the highest point or the best price. The zeros are when something lands or when profit breaks even. The y-intercept is where it all starts. Today's job is translating each feature into a sentence that answers a real question — and noticing which answers don't make sense.

Today you'll solve and graph real-world quadratic problems, interpret key features in context, and decide which part of the domain is realistic.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Solve real-world problems modeled by quadratic functions
  • 2Interpret the vertex, intercepts, and zeros in context
  • 3Identify a realistic domain and reject non-viable solutions

Vocabulary

Two ideas for real problems

Projectile Model

h(t) = −16t² + v₀t + h₀ in feet (or −4.9t² + v₀t + h₀ in meters), where v₀ is the starting upward speed and h₀ is the starting height.

💡Example: h(t) = −16t² + 48t + 5 means launched at 48 feet per second from 5 feet up.
Realistic Domain

The inputs that make sense in the situation — often the time from launch until landing, or prices that aren't negative.

💡Example: for a ball that lands at t = 3, the realistic domain is 0 ≤ t ≤ 3.

How It Works

Translate, solve, check

1
Identify what each variable means
Input: time, price, or length. Output: height, revenue, or area.
2
Match the question to a feature
Maximum or minimum → vertex. When it reaches 0 → zeros. Starting value → y-intercept.
3
Solve with the best method
Vertex with x = −b/(2a); zeros by factoring, square roots, or the quadratic formula.
4
Check that the answer makes sense
Reject negative times, negative lengths, or anything outside the realistic domain.

💭 Notice: The quadratic formula often gives one negative time. It solves the equation correctly; that moment just didn't happen in the situation.

Real-World Context

Sports, engineering, business

Quadratic models show up in the arc of a basketball, the shape of a suspension cable, and the price that brings in the most revenue.

Example: A company's profit is P(x) = −2x² + 24x − 40 thousand dollars when it sells x hundred items. The vertex at x = 6 gives the maximum profit, P(6) = $32 thousand, and the zeros x = 2 and x = 10 are the break-even points.

Worked Example · Projectile

A ball thrown from 6 feet: h(t) = −16t² + 40t + 6

Goal: Find the maximum height and when the ball lands.

1
Maximum height
t = −b/(2a) = −40/(−32) = 1.25 s, and h(1.25) = −25 + 50 + 6 = 31 feet
2
When it lands
Solve −16t² + 40t + 6 = 0 with the formula: t ≈ 2.64 s or t ≈ −0.14 s
3
Interpret
Reject the negative time. The ball peaks at 31 feet after 1.25 seconds and lands about 2.64 seconds after the throw. Realistic domain: 0 ≤ t ≤ 2.64.
h(t) = −16t² + 40t + 6

Worked Example · Maximum Area

Fencing a garden against a wall

Goal: 60 feet of fencing makes three sides of a rectangle; a wall is the fourth side. What dimensions give the most area?

1
Write the model
Two sides of length x and one of 60 − 2x: A(x) = x(60 − 2x) = −2x² + 60x
2
Find the vertex
x = −60/(2 · −2) = 15, and A(15) = 15 · 30 = 450
3
Interpret
Two 15-foot sides and a 30-foot side along the wall give the maximum area, 450 square feet. Realistic domain: 0 < x < 30.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

A rocket's height is h(t) = −16t² + 64t feet.

Question 1 — When does the rocket reach its maximum height?

Here's why: t = −b/(2a) = −64/(−32) = 2. t = 4 is when it lands, since −16t(t − 4) = 0.

Question 2 — What is the maximum height?

Here's why: h(2) = −16(4) + 64(2) = −64 + 128 = 64 feet. 128 is just the 64t term, without subtracting 16t².

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
A ball's height is h(t) = −16t² + 32t + 48. What does the y-intercept, 48, mean?
Here's why: The y-intercept is h(0), the height at time 0 — where the ball starts.
Question 2
For h(t) = −16t² + 32t + 48, when does the ball land?
Here's why: −16(t² − 2t − 3) = −16(t − 3)(t + 1) = 0, so t = 3 or t = −1. Time can't be negative, so the ball lands at t = 3.
Question 3
For the same ball, what is its maximum height?
Here's why: The vertex is at t = −32/(−32) = 1, and h(1) = −16 + 32 + 48 = 64 feet.
Question 4
Revenue is R(p) = −5p² + 200p dollars, where p is the price. Which price maximizes revenue?
Here's why: p = −b/(2a) = −200/(−10) = 20. The maximum revenue is R(20) = $2,000; $40 is a zero, where revenue drops back to $0.
Question 5
A model gives a garden side length of x = 12 or x = −5 feet. What should you report?
Here's why: Both numbers solve the equation, but only 12 is viable. A negative length isn't possible, so −5 is rejected.
