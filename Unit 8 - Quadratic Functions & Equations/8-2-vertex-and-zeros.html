Let's Begin

Three forms, three shortcuts

The same parabola can be written three ways, and each form shows something for free. Vertex form shows the vertex. Factored form shows the zeros. Standard form shows the y-intercept. Knowing which form you have tells you where to look.

Today you'll pull the vertex and zeros straight out of an equation — no table needed — and interpret them in context.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Read the vertex from vertex form and the zeros from factored form
  • 2Find the vertex of a quadratic in standard form using x = −b/(2a)
  • 3Interpret the vertex and zeros in a real-world context

Vocabulary

Two forms that show their secrets

Vertex Form

f(x) = a(x − h)² + k, with vertex (h, k).

💡Watch the sign: y = (x − 3)² + 2 has vertex (3, 2), but y = (x + 3)² + 2 has vertex (−3, 2).
Factored Form

f(x) = a(x − p)(x − q), with zeros x = p and x = q.

💡Example: y = (x − 1)(x + 5) has zeros x = 1 and x = −5.

How It Works

Match the form to the feature

1
Vertex form → vertex
In a(x − h)² + k the vertex is (h, k). Flip the sign inside the parentheses.
2
Factored form → zeros
Set each factor equal to 0. The vertex's x-value is halfway between the zeros.
3
Standard form → vertex
For ax² + bx + c, the vertex's x-value is −b/(2a). Substitute it to get the y-value.
4
Interpret
In context, the vertex is the maximum or minimum, and the zeros are where the quantity equals 0.

💭 Notice: x = −b/(2a) works because the axis of symmetry is always halfway between the zeros — even when the zeros aren't whole numbers.

Real-World Context

Reading a punt

A coach looking at a punt's height formula wants two things: how high it goes (the vertex) and when it lands (a zero). Each form of the equation makes one of those easy to read.

Example: A punt's height is h(t) = −16(t − 2)² + 64 feet. The vertex (2, 64) means the ball peaks at 64 feet after 2 seconds. Factored, h(t) = −16t(t − 4), so it lands at t = 4 seconds.

Worked Example · Standard Form

Finding the vertex of f(x) = 2x² − 8x + 5

Goal: Find the vertex without a table.

1
Identify a and b
a = 2, b = −8
2
Find the x-value
x = −b/(2a) = 8/4 = 2
3
Find the y-value
f(2) = 2(4) − 16 + 5 = −3. The vertex is (2, −3), a minimum because a > 0.
f(x) = 2x² − 8x + 5, vertex (2, −3)

Worked Example · Factored Form

Zeros and vertex of f(x) = (x + 1)(x − 5)

Goal: Find the zeros and the vertex.

1
Zeros
x + 1 = 0 → x = −1, and x − 5 = 0 → x = 5
2
The vertex's x-value
Halfway between the zeros: (−1 + 5) ÷ 2 = 2
3
The vertex's y-value
f(2) = (3)(−3) = −9. The vertex is (2, −9).

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

f(x) = −(x + 4)² + 7

Question 1 — What is the vertex?

Here's why: Vertex form is a(x − h)² + k. Here x + 4 = x − (−4), so h = −4 and k = 7: the vertex is (−4, 7).

Question 2 — Is the vertex a maximum or a minimum?

Here's why: a = −1 is negative, so the parabola opens downward and the vertex is its highest point: a maximum of 7.

Why This Matters

The two questions that matter most

Finding the vertex and zeros straight from the equation is the fastest way to answer the questions that usually matter most: what's the best possible value, and when does the quantity hit zero?

This shows up everywhere

  • Maximum height and landing time of a projectile.
  • Maximum revenue from a pricing model.
  • Break-even points, where profit equals zero.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
What is the vertex of y = (x − 5)² − 2?
Here's why: a(x − h)² + k has vertex (h, k). Here h = 5 and k = −2.
Question 2
What are the zeros of y = 3(x − 2)(x + 7)?
Here's why: Set each factor equal to 0: x − 2 = 0 gives 2, and x + 7 = 0 gives −7. The 3 doesn't produce a zero.
Question 3
What is the x-coordinate of the vertex of y = x² + 10x + 3?
Here's why: x = −b/(2a) = −10/2 = −5.
Question 4
What is the vertex of y = −x² + 6x − 4?
Here's why: x = −b/(2a) = −6/(−2) = 3. Then y = −9 + 18 − 4 = 5, so the vertex is (3, 5).
Question 5
A rocket's height is h(t) = −16(t − 3)² + 144 feet. What does the vertex tell you?
Here's why: The vertex is (3, 144), and since a < 0 it's a maximum: 144 feet, reached at t = 3 seconds. The rocket lands later, at t = 6.
